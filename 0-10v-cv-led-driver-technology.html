0-10V CV LED Driver Technology

How 0-10V constant voltage drivers and low-voltage dimmers bring simple analogue dimming to 12V and 24V LED strip, and how to choose the right one for your project.

Overview

What is a 0-10V CV LED driver?

A 0-10V constant voltage (CV) driver is a 12V or 24V DC LED power supply with an analogue dimming input. A 0-10V control signal on a separate pair of low-voltage wires sets the brightness of the LED strip, linear profile or signage module connected to it: 10V gives full output and lower voltages dim the LEDs smoothly to minimum or off.

Our range includes mains-input drivers from Snappy, LTECH and Meanwell, from 36W to 600W, many with multi-mode inputs that also accept 1-10V, PWM, resistor, Triac or push dimming. For larger or existing installations, LTECH low-voltage 0-10V dimmers add dimming after a standard 12V or 24V power supply, with up to 480W across four channels.

  • Simple analogue dimming for 12V and 24V LED strip
  • Flicker-free PWM dimming with stable colour
  • Multi-mode 0/1-10V, PWM, Rx, Triac and push inputs
  • Colour temperature (CT) models for tuneable white strip
  • Low-voltage dimmers for use with existing power supplies

The technology

How 0-10V CV dimming works

A 0-10V CV driver takes mains power and produces a steady 12V or 24V DC output for the strip. The control pair, marked DIM+ and DIM-, carries a 0-10V signal from a wall controller, sensor or building management system.

To dim, the driver uses pulse width modulation (PWM): the output stays at full voltage but is switched on and off at high frequency, and the control level sets the on-time in each cycle. The strip dims smoothly while its colour stays consistent.

Alternatively, a low-voltage 0-10V dimmer can sit between a standard CV power supply and the strip, applying the same PWM dimming to the DC output.

Which approach?

0-10V CV driver or low-voltage dimmer?

Both dim 12V and 24V LED strip by PWM. The choice depends on the load size, layout and whether a power supply is already installed.

Slim IP20 drivers for luminaires and IP67 Meanwell HLG-B drivers for outdoor and wet locations.

Selection & set-up

Choosing and installing a 0-10V CV driver

  • Match the voltage: use a 12V driver for 12V strip and a 24V driver for 24V strip.
  • Size the power: add up the strip load (W/m × length) and allow at least 20% headroom.
  • Check the dimmer rating: with a low-voltage dimmer, stay within its current per channel as well as the power supply rating.
  • Choose the inputs: confirm the driver accepts the controller's signal, such as 0-10V, 1-10V or PWM.
  • Wire the control pair: DIM+ to DIM+ and DIM- to DIM-, following the manufacturer's and local wiring rules.
  • Limit voltage drop: on long runs, feed the strip from both ends or from several points.

FAQ

0-10V CV driver questions

A 0-10V constant voltage (CV) driver is a 12V or 24V DC LED power supply with an analogue dimming input. A 0-10V control signal on a separate pair of wires sets the brightness of the LED strip connected to it, from full output at 10V down to minimum or off.

The driver keeps its output at 12V or 24V and dims by pulse width modulation (PWM), switching the output on and off at high frequency. The 0-10V control level sets how long the output is on in each cycle, which keeps the colour of the LEDs consistent as they dim.

A low-voltage dimmer, such as the LTECH LT-704-5A or LT-DIN-711-12A, sits between a standard 12V or 24V constant voltage power supply and the LED strip. It takes the 0-10V control signal and dims the DC output by PWM, so an existing non-dimmable power supply can be used for 0-10V dimming.

Yes. A colour temperature (CT) driver such as the LTECH LM-G2A2 series has two output channels for the warm and cool white LEDs, and uses one 0-10V input for brightness and a second for colour temperature.

Match the driver output voltage to the strip, then add up the strip load in watts (watts per metre multiplied by length) and choose a driver rated at least 20% above that total. For long runs, feed the strip from more than one point to limit voltage drop.
