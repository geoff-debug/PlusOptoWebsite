Compact CV LED Driver Technology

How compact constant voltage LED drivers power LED strip and modules indoors, and how to choose the right voltage, power and installation for even, reliable light.

Overview

What is a compact CV LED driver?

A compact constant voltage (CV) LED driver converts mains AC into a fixed, regulated low-voltage DC supply, typically 12V, 24V or 48V, in a small plastic case for indoor use. The driver holds the voltage steady and the connected LED strip or modules draw the current they need, up to the driver's maximum rating.

CV drivers power LED products that have their own built-in current control, such as flexible and rigid LED strip, neon flex and chain modules. Our compact range from Snappy, Philips and Lifud covers power ratings from 8W to 320W, with IP20 cases for dry indoor locations and Class II models for independent, remote installation.

  • Fixed 12V, 24V or 48VDC output to match your LED products
  • Power ratings from 8W to 320W
  • Compact IP20 plastic cases for indoor installations
  • Class II models for independent use
  • Short circuit and over-current protection

The technology

Voltage, load and voltage drop

A CV driver holds a fixed output voltage. LED strip and modules connect to it in parallel, and the total load is the sum of every connected run. Keep that total to no more than about 80% of the driver's rating for cool, reliable operation.

Current flowing along cables and the thin copper tracks of LED strip causes voltage drop, so LEDs at the far end of a long run receive less voltage and look dimmer. For the same power, a higher voltage carries less current, so 24V and 48V strips can run further than 12V before brightness falls away.

Feeding long runs from both ends or the middle, and using thicker cable between driver and strip, also keeps brightness even.

12V vs 24V vs 48V

Choosing the output voltage

The driver voltage must always match the LED product. Connecting 12V strip to a 24V driver will damage it, and 24V strip on a 12V driver will barely light.

When you are choosing both strip and driver, higher voltage halves the current for the same power each time it doubles, reducing voltage drop and allowing longer runs from a single feed.

Installation

Installing compact CV drivers

  • Match the voltage of driver and LED product exactly.
  • Size for 80% load: total LED wattage divided by 0.8 gives the minimum driver rating.
  • Mount in a dry, ventilated position away from insulation and heat sources; IP20 drivers are for indoor use only.
  • Keep low-voltage cables short and use a suitable cross-section to limit voltage drop.
  • Feed long runs at both ends or in the middle, and split large loads across several drivers if needed.
  • Use Class II drivers with cable covers for independent installation outside a luminaire.

Sizing example

4 metres of 24V LED strip at 12W per metre:

  • Strip load: 4m x 12W = 48W
  • Minimum driver for 80% load: 48W / 0.8 = 60W
  • Suggested driver: 60W or above, 24VDC

FAQ

Compact CV driver questions

A compact constant voltage (CV) LED driver converts mains AC into a fixed, low-voltage DC output such as 12V, 24V or 48V, in a small plastic case for indoor use. It powers LED strip, chain modules and other products with built-in current control, which draw the current they need from the fixed voltage.

Voltage drop is the loss of voltage along cables and the copper tracks of LED strip, which makes the far end of a long run dimmer. Reduce it by using a higher voltage (24V or 48V instead of 12V), keeping runs within the strip's maximum length, feeding long runs from both ends or the middle, and using thicker cable between driver and strip.

As a rule, keep the connected LED load to no more than about 80% of the driver's rated power. This keeps the driver cooler, extends its life and allows for tolerances in LED power. For a 48W load, choose a driver rated at 60W or more.

A Class II driver has double or reinforced insulation, so it does not need an earth connection and can be used independently, outside a luminaire. Drivers rated for independent use can be installed remotely, for example in a ceiling void or cabinet, without an additional enclosure.

No. Compact drivers rated IP20 are for dry indoor locations only. For outdoor, damp or wet locations use an IP67 waterproof driver, available in plastic or metal cases.
