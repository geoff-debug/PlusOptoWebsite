RF Receiver Driver Technology

How RF receiver drivers give wireless control of LED strip from hand-held remotes and touch panels, and how to choose the right one.

Overview

What is an RF receiver driver?

An RF receiver driver (also called a receiving controller) sits between a power supply and the LED strip. It receives wireless commands from a hand-held remote or wall touch panel and dims each output channel by pulse width modulation (PWM). Depending on the model and how it is wired, it controls single colour, tuneable white (CCT), RGB, RGBW or RGB+CCT strip, with no control cable between the remote and the lights.

Our range from Mi-Light, MiBoxer, Ltech and Sunricher covers 1 to 5 channel receivers. Most run from a 5–24VDC or 12–24VDC supply with constant voltage outputs, and constant current versions drive LED fittings at 350, 700 or 1050mA. Combo models such as the MiBoxer CL series have a mains power supply built in, and some receivers add WiFi app control or a DMX interface.

  • Wireless control of LED strip from remotes and touch panels
  • Single colour, CCT, RGB, RGBW and RGB+CCT models
  • Zone control and synchronised effects across receivers
  • Constant voltage and constant current outputs
  • WiFi app and voice control on FUT W+ models

The technology

How RF receiver drivers work

The remote or touch panel sends a short radio message, usually in the 2.4GHz band, carrying its own ID, the zone and the command. A receiver only responds to IDs it has learned during pairing, so neighbouring systems do not interfere.

The receiver switches each output channel on and off at high frequency using PWM. Changing the on-time of the red, green, blue, white or warm and cool white channels sets the brightness and colour of the strip.

Receivers can relay and synchronise: on many 2.4GHz systems a receiver passes the signal on to others within range, so colour changing effects stay in step across long runs.

Benefits

Advantages of RF receiver drivers

Why RF control is a simple, low-cost way to add colour and dimming to LED strip.

No control wiring

Remotes and battery or mains touch panels link to receivers by radio, with no cables to the lights.

Full colour control

Dimming, tuneable white, RGB, RGBW and RGB+CCT, often from the same multi-mode receiver.

Zones and sync

Control areas separately from one remote, or run effects in step across many receivers.

App and voice

FUT W+ receivers and gateways add smartphone app control and Alexa or Google voice control.

CV and CC

Drive LED strip or constant current fittings from the same family of remotes and panels.

DMX ready

Ltech F4-DMX and F5-DMX receivers also accept wired DMX512 for larger installations.

Selection & set-up

Choosing and installing an RF receiver

  • Pick the system first: choose the remote or panel you want, then a receiver from the same brand and RF band.
  • Match the channels: 1 channel for single colour, 2 for CCT, 3 for RGB, 4 for RGBW and 5 for RGB+CCT.
  • Check the current: keep each channel within its rating, and the total within the receiver's maximum load.
  • Size the supply: the power supply must match the strip voltage and cover the full load with at least 20% headroom.
  • Pair each receiver: use the learning button or power-on method in the datasheet to link it to the remote or zone.
  • Mind the range: typically 30m in open space; metal enclosures and walls reduce it, so mount receivers where the signal can reach.

FAQ

RF receiver driver questions

An RF receiver driver sits between a low voltage power supply and the LED strip. It receives wireless commands from a hand-held remote or wall touch panel and dims each output channel by PWM, so single colour, tuneable white, RGB, RGBW and RGB+CCT strip can be controlled with no control wiring.

Each receiver learns the ID of its remote or touch panel. Typically you press the learning button on the receiver, or switch its power on, and then press a button or zone on the remote within a few seconds; the lights flash to confirm. The exact steps are in each receiver's datasheet, and the same steps with a long press clear the pairing.

Zones let one remote control several receivers or groups separately, for example kitchen and lounge strip on different zone buttons. Sync makes several receivers in the same zone run colour changing effects in step, so long runs fed by more than one receiver change together.

Use a constant voltage (CV) receiver for standard 12V or 24V LED strip, which is powered at a fixed voltage. Use a constant current (CC) receiver, such as the Ltech T3-CC, T4-CC, R4-CC or F4-CC set to 350, 700 or 1050mA, for LED fittings and modules that need a fixed current.
