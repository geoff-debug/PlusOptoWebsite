0-10V LED Driver Technology

How 0-10V dimmable constant current LED drivers use a simple analogue control signal to dim LED luminaires, and how to choose between 0-10V, 1-10V, multi-mode and tuneable white drivers.

Overview

What is a 0-10V LED driver?

A 0-10V dimmable LED driver is a constant current LED power supply with an analogue dimming input. A low-voltage DC control signal, carried on a separate pair of wires, sets the light output: 10V gives full brightness, and lowering the voltage dims the LEDs smoothly down to their minimum level or off. It is one of the simplest and most widely used ways to dim commercial LED luminaires.

Our 0-10V CC range from Lifud and LTECH, plus the SRP-2007 50W driver, covers 8W to 50W, with output currents from 100mA to 1500mA set by DIP switch or NFC programming. Multi-mode drivers also accept PWM and variable resistor (Rx) signals, and LTECH's dual-channel models add 0-10V tuneable white control.

  • Simple analogue dimming from a wall controller, sensor or BMS
  • Smooth, flicker-free dimming to 1% (0.01% on LTECH T-PWM models)
  • 0-10V, 1-10V, PWM and Rx inputs on multi-mode drivers
  • Output current set by DIP switch or NFC
  • Tuneable white dual-channel options

The technology

How 0-10V dimming works

Each driver is connected to mains power and, separately, to a two-wire control pair marked DIM+ and DIM-. The driver's dimming input supplies a small current, and the controller sinks it to pull the control voltage down between 10V and 0V.

The driver reads that voltage and reduces its constant current output in proportion: around 10V gives 100% output, and the light dims as the voltage falls. On dim-to-off models the LEDs switch off at about 0.5V; on 1-10V models the light holds at minimum below 1V.

Because the signal is analogue, every driver on the same control pair dims together. Different zones need their own control channels.

Advantages of 0-10V LED drivers

Why 0-10V remains a popular choice for dimming commercial LED lighting.

Simple to install

Smooth dimming

Flicker-free dimming to 1%, or down to 0.01% with LTECH T-PWM technology.

Wide compatibility

Works with wall dimmers, occupancy and daylight sensors, and building management systems.

Cost effective

Tuneable white

Flexible set-up

Set the output current by DIP switch, or by NFC from a phone app without powering the driver.

Selection & set-up

Choosing and installing a 0-10V driver

  • Match the current: set the output current by DIP switch or NFC to the LED module's rated current.
  • Check the voltage window: the LED forward voltage must sit within the driver's output voltage range.
  • Choose the signal: 0-10V dim-to-off, 1-10V, or a multi-mode driver to suit the controller.
  • Size the controller: check how many drivers it can sink current for on one control pair.
  • Wire the control pair: DIM+ to DIM+ and DIM- to DIM-, kept separate from the mains wiring as the instructions require.
  • Zone the lighting: use a separate control channel for each group that needs its own level.

FAQ

0-10V driver questions

0-10V is an analogue lighting control method. A low-voltage DC signal between 0 and 10 volts, carried on a separate pair of control wires, sets the light output of the driver: 10V gives full brightness and lower voltages dim the LEDs, down to minimum or off.

With 1-10V, the driver dims to its minimum level at 1V and below, and the light is switched off by breaking the mains supply. With 0-10V dim-to-off drivers, the light turns off when the control signal falls to around 0.5V or below, so no separate switching is needed. Many modern drivers recognise both signals automatically.

Several drivers can be wired in parallel on the same control pair, and they all dim together. The number depends on how much current the controller can sink compared with the current each driver's dimming input supplies, so check both datasheets. 0-10V is not addressable, so drivers that need different levels need separate control channels.
