Transflective TFT Technology

Colour transflective TFT LCD modules from 1.75" to 5.0", readable indoors and in direct sunlight. Find out how a transflective display uses ambient light, and how to choose a size, driver IC and interface.

Overview

A standard TFT LCD is transmissive: all of its light comes from the backlight, so in bright sunlight the image can wash out. A transflective TFT adds a part-reflective layer behind the liquid crystal. Indoors the backlight lights the image as normal, and outdoors the display also reflects sunlight back through the pixels, so the brighter the light, the clearer the image.

That makes transflective TFTs a good fit for outdoor industrial devices, vehicle GPS displays and handheld equipment, where the display must stay readable in changing light without a power-hungry backlight. How a Transflective TFT Works

Like any TFT LCD, each pixel is a liquid crystal light valve with a red, green or blue colour filter, switched by its own thin film transistor. The difference is the layer behind the liquid crystal.

In a transflective panel that layer is part reflector and part window. Light from the backlight passes through the window areas, while ambient light coming in from the front bounces off the reflector areas and back out through the same pixels. Both light paths pass through the colour filter, so the image keeps its colours whichever light is doing the work.

Advantages of Transflective TFTs

Why designers choose transflective TFTs for displays used outdoors and on the move

Sunlight Readable

Reflected sunlight lights the image, so the display gets clearer rather than washing out as the light gets brighter.

Lower Power

With ambient light doing more of the work, the backlight can run lower outdoors, saving power in battery equipment.

Indoors and Outdoors

The backlight still lights the image indoors and at night, so one display works in every lighting condition.

Wide Viewing Angles

The 5.0" uses an IPS panel, with wide viewing angles in both transmissive and reflective modes.

Wide Temperature

The 5.0" operates from -30℃ to +85℃, suiting vehicles and outdoor enclosures.

Flexible Interfaces

MCU, SPI and RGB interfaces to suit anything from a small microcontroller to an embedded processor.

Transmissive, Reflective or Transflective?

A transmissive display relies on its backlight, so it is bright indoors but can wash out in sunlight. A reflective display uses only room light, so it reads well in the sun but cannot be seen in the dark.

A transflective display combines the two, giving a readable image in every condition from a dark cab to direct sunlight.

Choosing an Interface

Small transflective TFTs such as the 2.4" have a driver IC with its own frame memory, so a microcontroller can write to it over an MCU parallel bus or SPI and only update what changes. Larger panels such as the 5.0" use an RGB interface, driven continuously by a processor with a display controller.

A transflective TFT LCD combines a transmissive display, lit by its own backlight, with a reflective one, lit by room light or sunlight. A part-reflective layer behind the liquid crystal lets backlight through and also reflects ambient light back through the pixels, so the display can be read indoors, in the dark and in bright sunlight.

A high brightness TFT stays readable in sunlight by using a much brighter backlight, which uses more power and makes more heat. A transflective TFT uses the sunlight itself: the brighter the light, the more is reflected, so the image holds up outdoors while the backlight can stay at a lower level.
