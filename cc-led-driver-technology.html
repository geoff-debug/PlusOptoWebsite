Constant Current LED Driver Technology

How constant current LED drivers power LED modules and light engines, and how to match current, voltage and power to choose the right driver.

Overview

What is a constant current LED driver?

A constant current (CC) LED driver converts mains power into a regulated DC supply that holds the current through the LEDs at a fixed value, such as 350mA, 700mA or 1050mA. The output voltage automatically adjusts to whatever the connected LEDs need, within the driver's rated voltage window.

LEDs are current-driven devices: their brightness, colour and lifetime depend on the current flowing through them, and a small change in voltage can cause a large change in current. Holding the current constant keeps light output stable and protects the LEDs. Our compact, non-dimmable CC drivers from Lifud, Snappy, LTECH and Philips Xitanium cover 4W to 60W, with output currents from 150mA to 1550mA.

  • Stable light output and colour from regulated current
  • Protects LEDs from over-current and thermal runaway
  • Compact housings for downlights, panels and luminaires
  • Flicker-free output on many models
  • Short circuit and over-current protection

The technology

Current, voltage window and power

Every CC driver has three key output ratings: the fixed output current, the output voltage window it can supply while holding that current, and the maximum power.

When LEDs are connected in series, the same current flows through each one and their forward voltages add up. If each LED has a forward voltage of around 3V, ten in series need about 30V. That total must fall inside the driver's voltage window, for example 25 to 42V.

The power drawn is simply current x voltage: 1050mA at 30V is about 31.5W, which must be within the driver's rated power.

Selection guide

Choosing a CC driver in five steps

  1. Find the LED current from the LED module or light engine datasheet, e.g. 700mA.
  2. Add up the forward voltage of all LEDs or modules connected in series.
  3. Check the voltage window: the total must sit within the driver's output range, ideally not at either end.
  4. Check the power: current x voltage must be within the driver's rating.
  5. Choose the format: input voltage, size, connection type, IP rating and whether dimming is needed.

Worked example

A downlight light engine rated at 700mA with a forward voltage of 33V:

  • Required current: 700mA
  • Load voltage: 33V
  • Power: 0.7A x 33V = 23.1W
  • Suitable driver: 700mA, window covering 33V, 25W+

FAQ

CC driver questions

A constant current (CC) driver holds the current at a fixed value, such as 350mA or 700mA, and lets the output voltage vary to suit the LEDs. It is used for LED modules, light engines and COBs without their own current control. A constant voltage (CV) driver holds a fixed voltage, such as 12V or 24V, and is used for LED strip and modules that have built-in resistors or current regulators.

Match the driver's output current to the current your LEDs are rated for, check that the total forward voltage of the LEDs falls inside the driver's output voltage window, and make sure the power (current x voltage) is within the driver's rating. Then choose the input voltage, connection type, size and IP rating to suit the luminaire.

The output voltage window is the range of voltages a CC driver can supply while holding its rated current, for example 25 to 42VDC. The combined forward voltage of the LEDs connected to it must sit within this range, ideally not right at either end.

Series connection is preferred, because the same current flows through every LED and each one runs at exactly the rated current. Parallel strings can share current unevenly unless the LED module is designed for it, so follow the module manufacturer's guidance.

A flicker-free driver supplies a smooth DC current with very little ripple, so the light output does not pulse. This improves visual comfort, avoids stroboscopic effects and prevents banding when the light is filmed or photographed.
