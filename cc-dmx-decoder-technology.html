CC DMX Decoder Technology

How constant current DMX decoders drive series strings of power LEDs at a fixed current, dimmed from a DMX512 controller.

Overview

What are CC DMX decoders?

A constant current DMX decoder controls LEDs that need a set current rather than a set voltage. It takes the DMX512 signal from the master and power from a DC supply, and drives each output at a fixed current such as 350mA or 700mA.

It suits power LEDs, spots, downlights and wall washers wired as series strings, with no driver of their own. Each channel is dimmed from 0 to 100% with PWM, so the LEDs keep the same colour as they dim. Our range covers 4-channel and 16-channel decoders, with fixed or DIP-selectable current.

  • DMX512 in, constant current PWM out
  • 4 or 16 channels
  • 350mA, 700mA or 300–1050mA selectable
  • 12–36V or 12–48V DC input
  • Dimming, CCT, RGB and RGBW control

CC decoders at a glance

  • Input signal: DMX512, RDM
  • Output: Constant current PWM
  • Channels: 4 or 16
  • Current: 300 to 1050mA
  • Supply: 12 to 48VDC
  • Dimming: 0–100%
  • Addressing: DIP, buttons, RDM
  • DMX ports: XLR, RJ45, terminal

The technology

How a CC DMX decoder works

Each output channel has a current regulator. It adjusts the voltage across the LED string so that the current stays at the set value, whatever the number of LEDs, within the decoder's output voltage range.

The decoder reads its channels from the DMX line, starting at its DMX address. Each value from 0 to 255 sets the PWM duty cycle: the regulated current is switched on and off rapidly, and the share of time it is on sets the brightness.

The supply voltage must be higher than the total forward voltage of each LED string, so the regulator has headroom to work.

Selection & set-up

Choosing and setting up a CC DMX decoder

  • Match the current: choose a decoder or setting that matches the LED's rated current.
  • Add up the voltage: multiply the LED forward voltage by the number in each string, and keep it within the output voltage range.
  • Choose the supply: pick a DC supply above the string voltage, within the decoder's input range, sized for all channels.
  • Set the current first: on the LT-858-CC, set the DIP switches with the power off.
  • Set the address: use DIP switches, the buttons or an RDM master.
  • Wire the line: daisy-chain the DMX to each decoder and terminate the last one with 120Ω.

FAQ

CC DMX decoder questions

A constant current DMX decoder takes a DMX512 signal and power from a DC supply, and drives each output channel at a fixed current, such as 350mA or 700mA. It suits power LEDs and LED modules wired in series strings, rather than 12V or 24V LED strip.

Use a CC decoder when the LEDs need a set current, for example 1W or 3W power LEDs, downlights, spots and wall washers without their own driver. Use a CV decoder for 12V or 24V LED strip and modules, which have their own resistors or current regulators built in.

The LEDs on each channel are wired in series, and their forward voltages add up. The total must sit within the decoder's output voltage range and below the supply voltage. For example, the LT-858-CC gives 3 to 42V from a 48V supply, so about 13 LEDs of 3.2V can run on each channel.

On the LT-858-CC, a set of DIP switches selects the current from 300mA to 1050mA in 50mA steps, set with the power off. The LT-880 and SR-2112B have a fixed current: 350mA on the LT-880-350 and SR-2112B, and 700mA on the LT-880-700 and SR-2114B.

The LT-858-CC and LT-880 use DIP switches 1 to 9, where the address is the sum of the switches turned on, and also support RDM addressing. The SR-2112B uses three buttons and a digital display to set the hundreds, tens and units of the address.
