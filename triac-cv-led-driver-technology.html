Triac CV LED Driver Technology

How Triac mains dimmable constant voltage drivers let 12V and 24V LED strip be dimmed from standard wall dimmers or push switches, and how to choose the right one.

Overview

What is a Triac CV LED driver?

A Triac dimmable constant voltage (CV) driver is a 12V or 24V DC LED power supply that works with a standard mains phase-cut dimmer. The dimmer is wired in the live feed in place of a light switch and cuts part of each mains half-cycle. The driver reads what remains and dims the LED strip, linear light or signage connected to its output, with no extra control wiring.

Our Triac CV range from Snappy, Lifud and LTECH covers 10W to 300W at 12V or 24V, in compact and slim linear cases. The drivers work with most leading and trailing edge dimmers for smooth, flicker-free dimming, and LTECH models add mains push dimming, dimming down to 0.1% and a dual-channel CCT version for tuneable white strip.

  • Dims 12V and 24V LED strip from a standard wall dimmer
  • No extra control wiring: ideal for retrofit and residential projects
  • Flicker-free PWM dimming with stable colour
  • Mains push dimming on LTECH models
  • Dual-channel CCT model for tuneable white strip

The technology

How Triac CV dimming works

A Triac (phase-cut) dimmer removes part of each mains half-cycle: a leading edge dimmer cuts the start, a trailing edge dimmer cuts the end. The driver measures the remaining conduction angle to work out the dimming level.

It then produces a steady 12V or 24V output and dims the strip by pulse width modulation (PWM), switching the output on and off at high frequency. The lower the dimmer setting, the shorter the on-time, while the strip's colour stays consistent.

On drivers with push dimming, a mains push switch on a separate terminal does the same job: a short press switches on or off, and a long press dims.

Selection & set-up

Choosing and installing a Triac CV driver

  • Match the voltage: use a 12V driver for 12V strip and a 24V driver for 24V strip.
  • Size the power: add up the strip load (W/m × length) and allow at least 20% headroom.
  • Use the right load: Triac CV drivers suit resistor-limited LED strip; check before connecting constant current fixtures such as MR16 lamps.
  • Use an LED-rated dimmer: keep the total driver load within its LED rating; trailing edge usually gives the smoothest results.
  • Set the minimum level: if the dimmer has a minimum-level adjustment, set it so the strip stays stable at the bottom of the range.
  • Limit voltage drop: on long runs, feed the strip from both ends or from several points.

FAQ

Triac CV driver questions

A Triac dimmable constant voltage (CV) driver is a 12V or 24V DC LED power supply that works with a standard mains phase-cut wall dimmer. The dimmer cuts part of each mains half-cycle, and the driver reads what remains and dims the LED strip connected to its output, with no extra control wiring.

The driver keeps its output at 12V or 24V and dims by pulse width modulation (PWM), switching the output on and off at high frequency. The setting of the Triac dimmer controls how long the output is on in each cycle, which keeps the colour of the strip consistent as it dims.

Push dimming uses a standard mains retractive push switch instead of a dimmer. A short press switches the light on or off and a long press dims up or down, with the last level remembered. Drivers such as the LTECH LM-G1T2 series accept either a Triac dimmer or a push switch.

Triac CV drivers are designed for resistor current-limited loads such as standard 12V and 24V LED strip. Fixtures with built-in constant current electronics, such as MR16 lamps or constant current strip, can draw a much higher inrush current and trip the driver's overload protection, so check compatibility before connecting them.

Match the driver output voltage to the strip, add up the strip load in watts (watts per metre multiplied by length) and choose a driver rated at least 20% above that total. Also check the total driver load sits within the Triac dimmer's LED rating.
