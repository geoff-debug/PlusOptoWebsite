Waterproof CV LED Driver Technology

How waterproof constant voltage LED drivers power LED strip and modules in outdoor and wet locations, and how to choose the right voltage, power and protection.

Overview

What is a waterproof CV LED driver?

A constant voltage (CV) LED driver converts mains AC into a fixed, regulated DC voltage, typically 12V, 24V or 48V. The connected LED strip or modules draw whatever current they need, up to the driver's rated maximum. CV drivers are used for products with their own built-in current control, such as LED strip, neon flex and chain modules.

A waterproof driver seals the electronics inside a case filled with potting compound, with sealed input and output leads, to achieve an IP67 rating. Our plastic case range from Snappy, PSU-LV and Meanwell covers outputs up to 240W at 12V, 24V and 48V, for outdoor signage, façades, landscape lighting and damp interiors.

The technology

How a CV driver powers LED strip

A CV driver holds its output at a fixed voltage. LED strip and modules are connected to it in parallel, so every run receives the same voltage and draws its own current. The total current, and therefore power, is the sum of all the connected loads.

To keep the driver cool and reliable, the connected load should normally be no more than about 80% of the driver's rated power. This headroom also allows for start-up current and higher ambient temperatures outdoors.

Inside a waterproof driver, the circuit board is encapsulated in potting compound, which keeps out moisture and dust and helps conduct heat to the case.

Protection

What IP67 means

RatingProtectionTypical location
IP20Fingers and large objects onlyDry indoor enclosures
IP65Dust tight, low-pressure water jetsSheltered outdoor
IP67Dust tight, temporary immersionOutdoor, damp and wet locations

The first digit is protection against solids and the second against water. A driver's IP rating only protects the installation if the cable connections are also made in suitably rated junction boxes or connectors.

Installation

Installing waterproof drivers outdoors

  • Size for 80% load: add up the LED wattage and choose a driver with at least 20% headroom.
  • Seal every connection in IP-rated junction boxes or waterproof connectors; the driver rating alone is not enough.
  • Add a drip loop to cables so water runs off before reaching the driver or connections.
  • Mount out of standing water and direct sunlight where possible, with space around the driver for cooling.
  • Keep low-voltage runs short or use thicker cable and higher voltage to limit voltage drop.
  • Choose a metal case for higher power, high ambient temperatures or more demanding sites.

Sizing example

5 metres of 24V LED strip at 14.4W per metre:

  • Strip load: 5m x 14.4W = 72W
  • Minimum driver for 80% load: 72W / 0.8 = 90W
  • Suggested driver: 100W, 24VDC, IP67

FAQ

Waterproof CV driver questions

A constant voltage (CV) LED driver converts mains power into a fixed DC voltage, such as 12V, 24V or 48V. The connected load draws whatever current it needs, up to the driver's maximum. CV drivers power LED strip, neon flex and chain modules that have their own built-in resistors or current regulators.

IP67 means the driver is fully protected against dust and can withstand temporary immersion in water. Waterproof drivers achieve this with a sealed case filled with potting compound and sealed flying leads, making them suitable for outdoor, damp and wet locations when connections are also protected.

Add up the total wattage of all LED strip or modules connected to the driver, then choose a driver rated so the load is no more than about 80% of its maximum. For example, 5 metres of 14.4W/m strip is 72W, so a 100W driver is a good choice. The driver's output voltage must match the strip voltage.

Always match the driver voltage to the LED product. Where there is a choice, higher voltages carry less current for the same power, so there is less voltage drop and longer runs can be powered from one feed. 24V is the most common choice for LED strip; 48V suits very long runs.

Plastic case drivers are light, compact, cost-effective and electrically insulated, ideal for lower and medium power installations. Metal case drivers dissipate heat better and are more robust, making them the choice for higher power, high ambient temperatures and demanding outdoor sites.
