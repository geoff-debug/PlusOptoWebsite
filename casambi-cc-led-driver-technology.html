Casambi CC LED Driver Technology

How Casambi enabled constant current drivers bring wireless Bluetooth control to LED downlights, spotlights and panels, and how to choose the right one.

Overview

What is a Casambi CC LED driver?

A Casambi enabled constant current (CC) driver is a mains LED driver with a Casambi Bluetooth Low Energy radio built in. It powers an LED module at a fixed current, and can be switched, dimmed and grouped from the Casambi app on a phone or tablet, or from Casambi compatible wireless switches and sensors. There is no control wiring between the driver and the controls, and no gateway is needed.

Our Casambi CC range from Philips, Lifud and TCI covers 13W to 62W with output currents from 100mA to 1200mA. Lifud and TCI drivers set the current with a DIP switch, and Philips Xitanium drivers use SimpleSet. Philips and TCI models also accept a standard push switch for local control.

  • Wireless control from the Casambi app, switches and sensors
  • Self-forming Bluetooth mesh: no gateway or control cables
  • Adjustable output current to suit the LED module
  • Smooth dimming down to 0.1% on Lifud models
  • Push switch input on Philips and TCI models

The technology

How Casambi control works

Every Casambi driver is a node in a Bluetooth Low Energy mesh. Each node relays messages to its neighbours, so a command from the app or a switch reaches every driver in the network, even those out of direct range of the phone.

The network is commissioned in the Casambi app: drivers are added, named and grouped into rooms, scenes and timers. The settings are stored in the network itself, so the lights keep working without the phone.

The driver then regulates a constant current into the LED module and dims it to the level requested. On models with a push input, a wired push switch adds local on/off and dimming.

Selection & set-up

Choosing and installing a Casambi CC driver

  • Match the current: choose a driver whose range includes the LED module's rated current, then set it by DIP switch or SimpleSet before switching on.
  • Check the voltage: the module's forward voltage must sit within the driver's output voltage range at that current.
  • Check the power: current × forward voltage must not exceed the driver's rating at that current setting.
  • Plan the mesh: keep drivers within Bluetooth range of each other, and avoid fitting them inside fully enclosed metal housings.
  • Commission in the app: add each driver to a Casambi network, then group lights and create scenes.
  • Add switches: use Casambi wireless switches, or a wired push switch on models with a push input.

FAQ

Casambi CC driver questions

A Casambi enabled LED driver has a Casambi Bluetooth Low Energy radio built in. It can be switched, dimmed and grouped from the Casambi app on a phone or tablet, or from Casambi compatible wireless switches and sensors, with no control wiring between the driver and the controls.

No. Each Casambi driver is a node in a Bluetooth mesh network, and every node passes messages on to its neighbours, so the system is commissioned and controlled directly from the app without a gateway. A gateway is only needed if you want to control or monitor the network remotely over the internet.

Lifud LF-ACB and TCI drivers set the output current with a DIP switch on the driver, from 100mA up to 1200mA depending on the model. Philips Xitanium Casambi drivers are set with Philips SimpleSet, which programs the current wirelessly before installation. Always set the current to match the LED module before switching on.

Yes. You can use a battery-free Casambi compatible wireless switch, or wire a standard retractive push switch to the push input of drivers such as the Philips Xitanium and TCI models. A short press switches the light on or off and a long press dims, alongside control from the app.

The Lifud LF-ACB series dims from 100% down to 0.1%, and the Philips Xitanium and TCI drivers dim from 100% down to 1%. The TCI drivers use amplitude (analogue current) dimming for ripple-free light, and the Lifud drivers meet the IEEE 1789 recommendations for flicker.
