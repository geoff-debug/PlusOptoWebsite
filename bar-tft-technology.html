Bar TFT Display Technology

Bar type TFT LCD modules from 2.86" to 12.3" with ultra-wide aspect ratios and resolutions up to 1920x720. Find out how bar displays work, how to choose an interface and where an elongated display makes the difference in product design.

Overview

A bar TFT display, also called a bar LCD or stretched display, is a full colour active matrix LCD with a long, narrow active area. Where a standard TFT has a 4:3 or 16:9 aspect ratio, bar displays run from around 2.5:1 to more than 5:1, so a single module can show status, controls and graphics along a slim strip of panel space where a conventional screen would be too tall to fit.

As the authorised distributor for Winstar Display Corporation we offer bar type TFT LCD modules from 2.86" to 12.3" with resolutions up to 1920x720. Most models use IPS panels for wide viewing angles, with SPI+RGB, RGB, MIPI DSI, LVDS or HDMI interfaces, high brightness options up to 1000 cd/m² and optional projected capacitive touch (PCAP) with optical bonding.

How a Bar TFT Works

Electrically, a bar TFT works like any other TFT LCD. A driver IC scans the rows and columns of the panel and your graphics are written into a frame buffer. What changes is the shape: an 8.8" bar display has the same diagonal as a small tablet, but at 480x1920 it is four times as long as it is tall.

Many bar panels are scanned along their long edge, which is why resolutions are often written as 480x1920 or 280x1424 rather than the other way round. To the driver the display is a tall, narrow portrait screen; mounted in landscape, your software simply rotates the graphics by 90°. The datasheet for each model confirms its native orientation.

Advantages of Bar TFT Displays

Why designers choose a bar display for products where a conventional screen would be too tall

Fits Narrow Spaces

A long, slim active area fits rack panels, 1U server fronts, dashboards and shelf edges where a 16:9 display simply would not fit.

More Information in One Strip

Show status, controls, media and messages side by side on a single module, replacing several small displays or indicator panels.

IPS Viewing Angles

Most models use IPS panels, with 80° viewing angles in every direction on many, so a long display reads evenly from end to end and from the side.

High Brightness Options

Versions from 800 to 1000 cd/m² keep graphics readable in bright retail, vehicle and outdoor-facing installations.

Touch Ready

Optional PCAP touch, with optical bonding on selected models for improved contrast and durability.

Easy Integration

From SPI+RGB and RGB for microcontrollers, through MIPI DSI and LVDS for application processors, to an 8.8" HDMI model with its own control board.

Choosing an Interface

The right interface depends on resolution, cable length and the processor in your design. Smaller bar displays use SPI+RGB or a parallel RGB bus, well supported by microcontrollers with an LCD controller. Higher resolution models use MIPI DSI or LVDS to connect to an application processor, with LVDS also suited to longer cable runs. For the quickest route to a working display, the HDMI model plugs straight into a PC or single board computer.
