LED Neon Flex Technology

How LED neon flex recreates the look of glass neon with a safe, low-voltage, flexible silicone light source, and how to choose between side view and top view versions.

Overview

What is LED neon flex?

LED neon flex, also called LED neon strip, is a flexible LED strip fully encapsulated in a solid, light-diffusing silicone profile. The LEDs sit at the base of the profile and the silicone spreads their light into a smooth, continuous, dot-free line, recreating the look of traditional glass neon without the glass, gas or high voltage.

Because it is flexible, it can be bent by hand into curves, outlines and lettering on site, and cut to length at marked points. Our white neon flex range runs at 24VDC using 2835 LEDs, in profiles from 4x8mm to 16x15mm, with seven colour temperature options between 2000K and 6500K, a 120° to 180° beam angle and IP67 protection. It is available in side view and top view versions, with 360°, multicolour and pixel-addressable options in the wider range.

  • Smooth, continuous, dot-free line of light
  • Safe 24VDC operation with no high-voltage transformers
  • Bends by hand and cuts to length on site
  • UV-stable silicone for indoor and outdoor use, rated IP67
  • Side view and top view bending to suit the shape of the design

The technology

Side view and top view explained

Inside every neon flex profile is a narrow LED strip. The direction the strip faces decides which face of the profile lights up, and which way the profile can bend.

In side view neon flex the LED strip stands on its edge, so light leaves through the narrow side of the profile. The profile bends horizontally, flat against the mounting surface, making it ideal for outlines, wall graphics and curved signage.

In top view neon flex the LED strip lies flat, so light leaves through the top face. The profile bends vertically, up and down, making it ideal for channels, coves, contours and lines that follow a rising or falling surface.

LED vs glass

Why LED neon flex replaces glass neon

LED neon flex delivers the same bold, continuous line of light that made glass neon popular, but runs at a safe low voltage, uses a fraction of the energy and will not shatter.

It can be cut, bent and installed by a lighting installer rather than a specialist glass bender, and colours or colour temperatures can be changed simply by choosing a different product.

Installation

Installing neon flex

  • Mount with clips or aluminium channel. Clips suit curves; channels keep straight runs neat and help spread heat.
  • Cut only at the marked cut points, then seal the end with an end cap or fit a feed connector.
  • Seal every cut end and joint with the matching cap or connector to maintain the IP67 rating outdoors.
  • Respect the bend direction: side view bends flat, top view bends up and down. Avoid tight kinks or twisting.
  • Size the power supply for the total wattage plus a safety margin, and use 24VDC drivers rated for the location.
  • Use IP68 products where the light will be continuously submerged; IP67 covers splashing and temporary immersion.

FAQ

LED neon flex questions

Side view neon flex emits light from its narrow side and bends horizontally, so it follows curves laid flat on a surface, such as outlines and wall graphics. Top view neon flex emits light from its top face and bends vertically, so it suits channels, coves and lines that rise and fall, such as stair nosings and contours.

LED neon flex gives a similar continuous line of light but runs at a safe low voltage, uses much less power, has no fragile glass or gas, and can be cut to length and bent on site. Glass neon needs high-voltage transformers and specialist glass bending.

Yes. Neon flex can be cut at the marked cut points, every 25mm or 50mm depending on the model in our white range. The cut end is then sealed with an end cap or fitted with a feed connector to maintain the IP rating.

Our white neon flex is rated IP67, which protects against dust, rain, splashing and temporary immersion, making it suitable for most outdoor and wet locations when correctly sealed. For continuous immersion, such as underwater, an IP68 product is needed.

Neon flex is usually fixed with mounting clips or pressed into aluminium channels, which hold the profile straight, help with heat dissipation and give a neat finish. Matching end caps and feed connectors seal each run.
